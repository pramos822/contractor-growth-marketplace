# Contractor Lead Follow-Up Checklist

## Quick answer

A contractor lead follow-up checklist should make sure every lead is contacted quickly, every quote gets a reminder, and every completed job triggers a review request. The system can be simple, but it must be used every day.

## Why contractors lose leads

Leads leak when:

- calls are missed
- website forms are answered too late
- quotes are sent without follow-up
- notes stay in someone’s phone
- no one owns the next step
- happy customers are not asked for reviews

## Daily checklist

### Morning

- Review new leads from the last 24 hours
- Contact every lead with no response yet
- Check urgent leads first
- Confirm today’s estimate appointments
- Review quotes that need follow-up

### Midday

- Check new form submissions
- Check missed calls
- Update lead statuses
- Send booking links if needed

### End of day

- Mark contacted leads
- Add tomorrow’s follow-up dates
- Send quote follow-up messages
- Add notes from calls
- Identify customers ready for review requests

## Quote follow-up checklist

After sending a quote:

- Follow up after 24 hours
- Follow up again after 3 days
- Follow up after 7 days if appropriate
- Mark the outcome clearly
- Save the objection or reason for delay

## Review request checklist

After a completed job:

- Confirm the customer is satisfied
- Send a short review request
- Include the review link
- Follow up once if appropriate
- Do not offer compensation for reviews

## Free tools

- Google Sheets for tracking
- Gmail templates for messages
- Tally Free for forms
- Google Calendar for reminders
- Loom Free for training the team

## KPI to track

Track these weekly:

- New leads
- Average response time
- Quotes sent
- Quotes followed up
- Jobs won
- Reviews requested
- Reviews received

## Final recommendation

Do not overcomplicate it. A simple checklist used daily beats expensive software nobody uses.
