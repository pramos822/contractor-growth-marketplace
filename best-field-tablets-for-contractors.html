Quick recommendation

Most small contractor teams should start with a durable mid-range tablet plus a rugged case instead of immediately buying expensive fully rugged hardware. Upgrade only if field damage, weather exposure, or daily use justifies the cost.

The best tablet is the one your crew will actually carry, charge, and use for photos, estimates, forms, and follow-up notes.

Features that matter

  • Durability: a good case may matter more than tablet specs.
  • Battery life: field crews need all-day use or easy truck charging.
  • Screen brightness: outdoor readability matters for roofs, trucks, and jobsite walkthroughs.
  • Camera quality: jobsite photos need to be clear enough for estimates and documentation.
  • Cellular option: helpful if crews work where Wi-Fi is not available.
  • App compatibility: verify CRM, forms, scheduling, photo storage, and estimating apps before buying.

Contractor use cases

  • Filling estimate request forms during walkthroughs.
  • Taking jobsite photos and adding notes immediately.
  • Accessing CRM, schedule, and customer info in the field.
  • Showing before/after photos or product options to customers.
  • Capturing signatures, approvals, and service notes.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Buying a tablet that does not support the field apps your team uses.
  • Skipping a rugged case and screen protection.
  • Buying too many devices before proving the workflow with one crew.
  • Ignoring charging, cellular data, and device management.
  • Choosing a model based only on price instead of total field usability.

Buying checklist

  • Compatible with CRM/forms/scheduling apps.
  • Rugged case and screen protector available.
  • Battery life suitable for field use.
  • Bright enough screen for outdoor work.
  • Camera good enough for documentation.
  • Cellular option if crews need always-on access.
