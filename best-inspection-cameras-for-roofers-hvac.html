Best Inspection Cameras for Roofers and HVAC Techs

Inspection cameras can help contractors document hard-to-see areas, explain issues to customers, and speed up estimates. This guide focuses on what to look for before buying — not hype.

Disclosure: Future product links may be affiliate links. This version uses neutral product-category guidance and official marketplace research links until affiliate accounts are approved. Always verify specs, warranty, safety suitability, and local work requirements before buying.

Quick recommendation

If you are buying your first inspection camera for roofing or HVAC work, start with a mid-range waterproof borescope/endoscope camera with a bright LED, semi-rigid cable, mobile/USB-C compatibility, and enough cable length for ducts, crawl spaces, and tight cavities.

Do not overpay for premium features until your team knows how often the camera will be used in estimates, inspections, documentation, and customer explanations.

Features that matter

  • Water resistance: helpful for wet areas, ducts, leaks, and dirty jobsite conditions.
  • Cable stiffness: semi-rigid cables are easier to guide through cavities than soft cables.
  • Lighting: adjustable LED brightness matters more than advertised resolution.
  • Camera diameter: smaller heads fit tighter spaces, but may compromise image quality.
  • Screen vs phone: dedicated screens are simpler for crews; phone cameras are cheaper and portable.
  • Recording/photos: useful for estimates, customer proof, and before/after documentation.

Roofing use cases

  • Checking hidden leak paths without opening more material than needed.
  • Documenting attic/cavity issues for customer education.
  • Looking into tight roof transitions, vents, and penetrations where access is limited.

HVAC use cases

  • Inspecting ductwork, coils, vents, drains, and hard-to-see areas.
  • Showing customers visible issues without relying only on verbal explanation.
  • Improving estimate notes with photos/video evidence.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Buying only by megapixels/resolution claims.
  • Choosing a cable that is too short for the actual work.
  • Ignoring app compatibility if using a phone-connected camera.
  • Using camera footage as a substitute for proper inspection standards.
  • Forgetting to clean and store the camera properly after dirty jobs.

Buying checklist

  • Waterproof or water-resistant camera head.
  • Semi-rigid cable with enough length for your jobs.
  • Adjustable LED lights.
  • Photo/video recording if you document estimates.
  • Compatible with your phone/tablet or has a dedicated screen.
  • Clear return policy and warranty.
